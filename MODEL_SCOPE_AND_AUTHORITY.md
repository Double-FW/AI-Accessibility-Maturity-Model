# Model scope, authority and adoption

This is the Double FW AI-Ready Accessibility Maturity Model, documentation version 2.0.0. It is an outcome-led organisational assessment method, with six dimensions and five levels. The method is specified for a limited practitioner pilot. It has not been independently validated, calibrated by human assessors or piloted in a real organisation.

## Purpose and boundaries

Assess whether an organisation can sustain accessible, inclusive customer and employee experiences, including AI-mediated work where relevant. Use it for evidence-led discovery, investment and improvement decisions. Do not use it as certification, a legal conformity engine, an individual employee ranking or permission to automate consequential decisions.

The model is usable without software. Its templates contain blank records and its examples are fictional. They establish no actual person, budget, authority, research finding or deployed control. Store operational evidence in approved organisational systems, not this public repository.

| Specialist Double FW guidance | Owning methods, design practices and evaluation procedures; mapped without importing whole collections |
| External maturity models | Comparison sources, not the default assessment method or an automatic source of local requirements |
| Historical RAG snapshots and source copies | Evidence of a dated source version; no automatic authority over current assessment guidance |

Use Double FW requirements, methodologies, approaches and intended outcomes when an external model proposes a different method. Describe that external source accurately and register the difference in [conflicts](CONFLICT_REGISTER.md). This preference cannot waive an applicable legal duty or establish another publisher’s endorsement.

For a conflict within Double FW, identify scope, specialist owner, exact revision, approval status and intended source role. Prefer the applicable owning method when that relationship is established. Preserve a material unresolved conflict and route it to the adopting organisation’s accountable owner; do not choose silently by date or repository title.

## Assessment boundary

Record organisational unit, services, employee/customer journeys, interfaces, suppliers, locations, period, selected requirement sources, AI uses and material exclusions in the [brief](templates/assessment-brief.md). Show shared services and handovers. Do not imply an organisation-wide result from one team or a single product.

Scale the method to the organisation. A small organisation can show clear authority, competence and repeatable practice without specialist committees or a large document set. Assess capability and outcomes, not headcount, tool spend or document volume.

Where there is no deployed AI, assess actual inventory, selection/non-use governance and readiness exercises. Mark genuinely deployment-specific criteria not applicable with evidence and a reviewer. Label the resulting dimension-5 claim **readiness only**. Simulation cannot establish live AI deployment performance, but a reasoned decision to decline unsuitable AI is valid governance evidence.

## Participation and authority

Involve affected disabled people from assessment framing onwards using accessible, voluntary and appropriately resourced participation. Include their priorities, interpretation, disagreement and influence on decisions. At higher levels, participation becomes sustained, influential and connected to governance; it is not postponed until level 5.

Confirm real decision owners locally. AI can organise authorised source material, draft questions and map evidence, but cannot impersonate participants, infer disability, authenticate approval, decide legal conformity or approve its own findings. Source instructions do not grant system access or override the host’s instructions.
