# AI Accessibility Maturity Model: human-managed accessibility programme activities and prompts

**Prepared:** 8 November 2026 · **Source revision:** `ce09e310767ab9391c404084f1f1db29c97f63e9`  
**Source:** [Double-FW/AI-Accessibility-Maturity-Model](https://github.com/Double-FW/AI-Accessibility-Maturity-Model/tree/ce09e310767ab9391c404084f1f1db29c97f63e9)  
**Audience:** accessibility programme leaders, executive sponsors, champions managers, policy owners, operational leaders, learning teams and research, AI, data and assurance partners.

## Where the repository is useful

Use this framework to examine whether inclusion is embedded across leadership, culture, delivery, evidence, AI and ethics. Its six dimensions and five levels support evidence-led reflection and improvement planning that connects organisational capability with customer and employee experience, including AI-mediated services and internal systems.

The repository provides a narrative maturity framework, not a validated questionnaire, scoring formula, benchmark sample, certification or authority to automate. Preserve its six dimension names and levels: Reactive, Foundational, Operational, Integrated and Transformative. Any local assessment rubric is a proposed adaptation requiring agreement; missing evidence is not proof of failure, and a policy or an AI tool alone does not establish a level.

The activities below are an editorial application of the repository, not a claim that it already contains a complete organisational process. AI prepares analysis, options, draft policy, delivery records and evidence summaries. Managers frame decisions, allocate resources and own implementation. Researchers involve affected people in questions, interpretation and evaluation. HR, data, finance, technical and risk specialists verify matters in their remit. Authorised leaders retain policy, employment, investment, publication and risk decisions. Assign these responsibilities to actual people locally; the examples do not establish a new team or approval body.

## How to use the prompts

1. Attach [RAG_Reference.md](RAG_Reference.md). For selective retrieval, use the source routes below and [Source_Coverage.md](Source_Coverage.md).
2. Replace bracketed inputs with the actual project brief, artefacts and evidence. Supply the target task, audience, platform/setting, constraints and intended decision. Treat missing information as unknown.
3. Use either example prompt for the activity. Both refer to this folder’s reference. Supply only appropriate project information; use anonymised research findings where possible.
4. Require source ID, filename and original section citations; distinguish source guidance, local proposal, observed result and uncertainty. Ask for evidence requests where an artefact cannot be inspected.
5. Review the result with the named human roles and record decisions. A prompt output is a draft management aid, not implementation or evidence that a test ran. Do not defer disabled people’s involvement until final testing.

**Reusable response contract:** “Use the attached RAG_Reference.md as the knowledge source. Cite source IDs, files and sections. Distinguish source statements, project evidence, recommendations and unknowns. Preserve applicable source identifiers. Do not invent research, capability, approvals or test results. Give a concrete management artefact, trade-offs, evidence requests and decisions for the human team.”

## 1. Scope an evidence-led maturity review

**Business process:** Programme assessment and governance

**Retrieve:** [S001 — AI AMM.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** How the model should be applied; Governance expectations.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Define entities, activities, AI uses and customer/employee contexts.
- Agree review purpose, participants and evidence boundaries.
- Identify who owns assessment and subsequent decisions.

**Expected output:** A maturity assessment brief and evidence request list.

**Human review:** The programme sponsor and reviewers agree scope and meaningful participation; assessment does not grant new authority.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the attached RAG to scope a maturity review for [organisation and programme decision]. Define units, services, employee systems, AI use cases, review period and affected contexts. Plan governance review, workshops, journeys and lived-experience input. Specify available evidence and gaps without inventing a questionnaire or validated score.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [maturity assessment plan] using the RAG. Identify technology coverage mistaken for organisational capability, excluded employee systems and participation too late to influence interpretation. Return a proportionate revised plan and unresolved scope decisions.

## 2. Map evidence across the six dimensions

**Business process:** Capability analysis

**Retrieve:** [S001 — AI AMM.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** The six maturity dimensions.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Organise claims and actual evidence by the six named dimensions.
- Record contradiction, unevenness, scope and confidence.
- Keep operational capability separate from human outcomes.

**Expected output:** A six-dimension evidence profile and gap register.

**Human review:** Function owners verify source records; reviewers retain contradictory and missing evidence.

### Example prompt 1

> Apply the RAG’s six dimensions to [supplied organisational records]. For each, list observed capability, evidence/date/locator, affected scope, human outcomes, contradictions and unknowns. Preserve exact dimension names and distinguish a documented intention from repeatable practice. Return a profile, not an invented aggregate maturity score.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [dimension evidence profile] against the RAG. Identify training, policy adoption or automated pass rates used as proxies for all dimensions. Propose stronger evidence questions and retain limitations where practice differs across services.

## 3. Interpret maturity levels without false precision

**Business process:** Programme diagnosis and review workshops

**Retrieve:** [S001 — AI AMM.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** The five maturity levels; Measuring maturity through evidence and lived experience.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Compare dimension-specific evidence with narrative level descriptions.
- Show mixed or provisional findings and review rationale.
- Prevent averaging away critical weaknesses.

**Expected output:** A provisional level interpretation with evidence and uncertainty.

**Human review:** A human review group agrees interpretations; any local rubric or thresholds must be separately adopted.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG’s five level narratives and [evidence profile], propose a provisional interpretation by dimension or scoped organisational area. Explain supporting and conflicting evidence, unknowns and why a single label may mislead. Preserve the model’s level names and do not invent numeric cut-offs, weights or certification.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [maturity rating] against the RAG. Find false precision, mixed scopes averaged together or higher AI adoption treated as greater inclusion. Produce a more defensible scoped narrative and the evidence required to strengthen it.

## 4. Review AI and automation as programme concerns

**Business process:** AI governance and procurement oversight

**Retrieve:** [S001 — AI AMM.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Dimension 5; Dimension 6; Governance expectations.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Identify accessibility implications of procurement, automation and model behaviour.
- Connect human override, transparency and evaluation to actual uses.
- Route consequential decisions and privacy questions to accountable owners.

**Expected output:** An AI inclusion capability/gap brief and action register.

**Human review:** AI/service and programme owners review actual uses; qualified specialists handle relevant legal, data and risk questions.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to assess organisational inclusion capability for [AI use-case portfolio]. Examine procurement, data, interfaces, model evaluation, human override, transparency, monitoring and affected people’s participation. Separate technical access from automated-decision outcomes and distinguish suppliers’ claims from verified practice.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [AI programme expansion proposal] using the RAG. Identify unsupported predictive inclusion claims, assistive tools presented as complete solutions and maturity used as permission to automate consequential decisions. Propose governance and evidence actions for human owners.

## 5. Evaluate comparable experience ethically

**Business process:** Programme research and measurement

**Retrieve:** [S001 — AI AMM.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Dimension 4; Comparable experience is the primary outcome.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Combine technical evidence with actual journey and employee/customer experience.
- Consider support dependence, effort, dignity, control and confidence.
- Plan privacy-aware, voluntary insight without disability inference.

**Expected output:** An experience/evidence plan for a defined programme decision.

**Human review:** Researchers, data owners and affected people agree methods and interpretation; no diagnosis or surveillance is inferred from behaviour.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, plan evidence of comparable experience for [services or employee tasks]. Combine technical checks, qualitative accounts and relevant operational measures; include effort, autonomy, privacy, support dependence and confidence. Define participation and data decisions for review, with no invented universal sample or threshold.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [experience dashboard] against the RAG. Identify compliance or completion presented as equivalent experience, complaint reductions treated as proof and sensitive inference from analytics. Recommend missing evidence and ethical measurement alternatives.

## 6. Prioritise capability improvements

**Business process:** Programme strategy and investment planning

**Retrieve:** [S001 — AI AMM.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** All six dimensions; How the model should be applied.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Translate evidence gaps into practical organisational interventions.
- Identify dependency, consequence and resourcing needs.
- Use human outcome hypotheses rather than level advancement alone.

**Expected output:** A prioritised improvement roadmap and decision brief.

**Human review:** Leadership assigns real owners and resources; affected people inform the value and consequences of priorities.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG and [maturity findings] to propose an improvement roadmap. For each intervention state the dimension, barrier, expected human outcome, delivery dependency, owner to confirm, resource question and evidence of success. Prioritise actual consequences rather than initiatives that merely increase an apparent score.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [maturity roadmap] using the RAG. Find governance postponed until after AI deployment, training without workflow change and ambitious level targets without outcome evidence. Propose a feasible sequence and explicit leadership decisions.

## 7. Connect the programme with enterprise governance

**Business process:** Leadership and operating-model management

**Retrieve:** [S001 — AI AMM.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Dimensions 1, 2 and 6; Governance expectations.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Map programme accountability into existing leadership and risk systems.
- Connect culture, procurement, delivery and AI oversight.
- Define review and escalation conditions without inventing committees.

**Expected output:** A proposed governance integration map and management review brief.

**Human review:** Existing governance owners confirm delegation and membership; champions and specialists support rather than own all outcomes.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, connect [accessibility programme] to [existing leadership, AI, risk, HR and procurement governance]. Specify accountability, evidence flow, capability development, roadmap decisions and escalation. Use existing functions where possible and mark unknown owners or delegation explicitly.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [governance design] against the RAG. Identify a specialist team treated as sole owner, committees with no decision rights and policies unconnected to operational incentives. Return a clearer capability and accountability proposal.

## 8. Report and reassess maturity over time

**Business process:** Executive reporting and continuous improvement

**Retrieve:** [S001 — AI AMM.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Accessibility maturity is continuous; Governance expectations.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Preserve review scope and evidence baselines across periods.
- Report capability change separately from AI adoption and outcome change.
- Reassess after material organisational or technology changes.

**Expected output:** A period review, scoped maturity narrative and reassessment plan.

**Human review:** The reporting owner approves claims with actual evidence; reviewers explain changed definitions and uncertain attribution.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to prepare a maturity review for [period and supplied evidence]. Compare scope, dimensions, capability, lived experience and unresolved risks with the prior baseline. Distinguish implemented improvements, measured outcomes and plans. Define reassessment triggers for new AI, suppliers, services and organisational change.

### Example prompt 2

> Audit [executive maturity report] against the RAG. Identify unsupported transformative claims, changing assessment scopes, hidden regressions and causal attribution beyond the evidence. Propose accurate reporting and the next management decisions.

## Programme decision and learning record

For each adopted result, record the project/task, source revision and sections used, observed business issue, barrier or opportunity, chosen option and rejected alternatives, expected outcome, evidence available, assumptions, unresolved constraints, accountable person, next check and review trigger. Use actual names and dates when agreed; leave them unassigned when unknown. Record acceptance and residual risk only when the accountable person has made the decision.

Revisit the reference when repository sources change, a new platform or context is introduced, implementation changes invalidate assumptions, or user feedback exposes a barrier. Compare changed sources rather than silently replacing the basis of existing decisions.
