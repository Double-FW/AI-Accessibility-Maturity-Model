# Measurement and comparable experience

Use this method with dimensions 3, 4, 5 and 6. It adds operational definitions to the framework’s comparable-experience ambition. It is not a validated questionnaire, legal test, universal equivalence threshold or statistical benchmark.

## Define the decision and actual task

State the task, people/context, product/service and version, relevant access method/settings, start conditions, intended correct end state and purpose of the comparison. Include discovery, authentication, permission, support, confirmation, changes and exit where needed. “Submit clicked” does not establish an accurate booking, payment or cancellation.

Agree local expectations and sampling rationale before collection with affected people and responsible specialists. Compare meaningful outcomes across methods, not everyone against an idealised sighted mouse user. Consider language, familiarity, learning, environment, fatigue, fluctuating needs and intersections without inferring diagnoses.

## Minimum measurement dictionary

| Outcome | Definition to agree | Required qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Navigation | Finding the necessary step, location and return route without losing context | Arrival alone is not orientation or understanding |
| Correct completion | Verified intended result among defined attempts, with non-entry, wrong results and abandonment retained | Show outcome categories and independent/chosen-support/forced-support attributes separately |
| Time and effort | Active time, waiting/support queues, rest, retries, recovery and reported demand | Faster is not universally better; preserve time to understand and choose |
| Choice and preference support | Discover, select, change, decline and where relevant retain/reverse settings and access methods | Check actual behaviour, privacy and meaningful alternatives |
| Comprehension/content | Relevant meaning, consequences and decision understanding in chosen formats | Recognition/accessible explanation may be more appropriate than memory recall |
| Continuity and recovery | Correct state and resumption after interruptions, errors, expiry and handovers | Keep lost work, duplicate actions and residual consequences visible |
| Dignity and autonomy | Participant accounts of control, respect and unnecessary dependence/disclosure | Chosen help can be a preference; forced help can expose exclusion |
| Satisfaction | Defined item/scale/anchors/timing plus open account and priorities | Report valid response count, non-response and context; it is not conformity or loyalty proof |
| Engagement and trust | Voluntary participation/exit and participant accounts of dependability and promise | More visits/time may mean friction; felt safety is not verified actual safety |

Each finding needs unit/scale, denominator, collection route, instrument/method version, period, context, evidence locator and limits. Use mutually exclusive primary outcome categories and separate assistance/barrier attributes to prevent double counting. Do not add overlapping research groups into a count of unique people.

## Participation, data and missingness

Use accessible invitations, consent materials, task instructions, alternatives, breaks, support and fair participation arrangements. Include people unable to enter or finish, not just successful end-of-journey respondents. Begin with open questions; the five POURS feelings are optional later prompts, never feelings assigned by an assessor.

Ask about voluntary task-relevant needs and preferences rather than requiring diagnosis. EAA Annex I VII criteria can inform task-condition research lenses where appropriate; their legal applicability is separate. Privacy is cross-cutting, not a respondent category. Do not infer disability from captions, keyboard or AT use.

Minimise collection, separate participation consent from the responsible organisation’s processing decisions, and review purpose, access, providers, locations, retention, withdrawal limits and reuse. Aggregation and pseudonymisation do not automatically remove identification risk. Keep recordings, health information, actual employment cases and complaints out of this public repository and unapproved model-training systems.

## Analysis and reporting

Preserve observations, participant accounts, inferences and proposals separately. Report non-entry, non-completion, missing data, non-response, negative cases and material differences alongside summaries. Protect small groups and quotations. A small qualitative study can reveal a serious barrier without estimating its prevalence.

Statistical inference requires an appropriate question, design and data, including necessary variance information. Before/after differences alone do not establish causation. Do not invent significance, population representativeness, emotional scores or universal acceptable timings from means/counts or synthetic personas.

A clean technical scan cannot establish comparable experience. Satisfaction cannot cancel an applicable failure; no complaints is not satisfaction. Preserve differences between restored access, satisfaction with recovery and unresolved consequences. Define local action thresholds with rationale and actual authority; the old timing bands in some historical sources are not adopted acceptance rules here.

## Source identities

Use the current [Double FW source crosswalk](DOUBLE_FW_DELIVERY_CROSSWALK.md). Preserve CP/CUX/MEM and CTL/ACT/AXE identities in owning methods; local AMM IDs do not replace them. POURS SX categories and EAA-guide IDSO outcomes are organisational guidance, not statutory clauses. A mapping points to a source; it does not prove an outcome.

Record the measurement decision, rights/data arrangements, findings and action through the [templates](templates/README.md). Sources may guide a method without authorising copying their material or disclosing actual participant records.
